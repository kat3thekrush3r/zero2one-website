Why it matters

If your chart of accounts is wrong, every report after it is too.

It's not about tracking everything — it's about tracking the right things clearly. A simple, well-structured income statement shows you what's actually happening; a bloated one hides it.

One sales account, not ten

Splitting revenue into a dozen channel accounts clutters your P&L. Keep a single sales account and use Classes to still see Shopify vs. Amazon vs. wholesale.

Keep COGS clean

Product and landed costs hit here as items sell. Keep adjustments and one-off write-offs out so your gross margin stays trustworthy.

Landed costs live on the balance sheet

Freight, duties, and brokerage sit in inventory and only hit COGS when the product sells — not the day you pay them.

Post to subaccounts, not headers

Book to 65010 Bank Charges, never the 65000 header. And no vague "miscellaneous" or "ask accountant" accounts.

Keep it clean

Four rules that keep a chart of accounts useful.

A structure is only as good as the discipline behind it. These habits keep your reports readable a year from now.

Every account earns its place

No "miscellaneous," "other," or "ask accountant." If you can't say in a sentence what belongs there, it shouldn't exist.

Post to subaccounts, not headers

Headers like 65000 Finance Expenses are for totals. Book the transaction to a subaccount — 65010 Bank Charges — so reports stay tidy.

Simplicity vs. insight

Merge accounts with the same purpose; split the big, decision-driving ones. Use Classes to slice by channel instead of duplicating accounts.

Retire dead accounts

Deactivate anything you no longer use so nothing posts there by accident and reports don't fill with empty lines.
